# August 18, 2026 Primary-Election Results Recap

**Research date:** August 19, 2026 (EDT)

## Scope confirmation

Associated Press identifies the August 18 contest group as primary voting in **Alaska, Florida, and Wyoming**, plus California’s **14th Congressional District special election** to fill the seat vacated by former Representative Eric Swalwell.[1]

The recap will distinguish winner projections and official/unofficial result returns from certified results. It will not describe an August 18 primary winner as a general-election winner or write it into the public general-election map.

## Initial platform reconciliation finding

The platform’s general-election race ledger still has no Florida, Alaska, or Wyoming primary winner populated for the August 18 statewide records. That is expected under the platform’s separation rule: the primary polling process may retain primary reporting separately but does not populate the public general-election result surface.

## Electoral-outcomes recap

The August 18 vote produced the first general-election matchups in major Florida races, narrowed Alaska’s nonpartisan fields, settled Wyoming’s Senate nominations, and yielded a close but complete preliminary count in California’s 14th District special election. These are **primary or preliminary special-election outcomes**, not certified general-election results.

| Jurisdiction | Confirmed outcome or current count | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Florida governor | Republican **Byron Donalds** and Democrat **David Jolly** won their respective nominations. | Donalds and Jolly advance to November. |
| Florida U.S. Senate special election | Republican **Ashley Moody** and Democrat **Angie Nixon** won their respective primaries. | Moody and Nixon advance to the general election to complete the remainder of Marco Rubio’s term. |
| Florida 7th District | **Ryan Elijah** defeated incumbent Republican **Cory Mills** in the Republican primary. | Mills’ two-term House tenure will end in January; Elijah proceeds to the general election. |
| Alaska U.S. Senate and House | AP reported Republican **Dan S. Sullivan**, Democrat **Mary Peltola**, and Republican Representative **Nick Begich III** advancing from Alaska’s top-four primary. | Alaska’s count remains unofficial; further positions and ballot processing continue under the state’s schedule. |
| Alaska governor | At the reported 80% precinct mark, **Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins**, **Tom Begich**, **Bernadette Wilson**, and **Dave Bronson** held the top four positions. | All four would advance under Alaska’s top-four system, but the count was still in progress and could change. |
| Wyoming U.S. Senate | Republican **Harriet Hageman** won the GOP nomination; Democrat **James Byrd** won the Democratic nomination. | Hageman and Byrd advance to November. |
| California 14th District special election | Alameda County’s **unofficial final** return reported **Aisha Wahab 33,399 (51.04%)** and **Melissa Hernandez 32,043 (48.96%)**, with all 323 vote-center precincts partially reported. | The contest remains a Democratic-held seat; the county labels the return preliminary/unofficial rather than certified. |

## Editorial significance

Florida supplied the evening’s clearest nomination changes: Donalds–Jolly sets the open-governor contest, Nixon’s victory establishes the Democratic Senate nominee, and Elijah’s win removed an incumbent from the Republican House ticket. Alaska’s top-four format requires more caution: early leaders are meaningful but not final until its later ballot-receipt and certification deadlines. California’s official county return is sufficiently complete to describe Wahab as leading the unofficial count, but the archive and public certified-results surfaces properly remain untouched until certification.

## Platform treatment

No August 18 primary outcome was inserted into the homepage’s general-election-only ticker, general-election map calls, or certified-results archive. That preserves the intended distinction between a nomination result, a preliminary special-election return, a DDHQ general-election call, and a certified result.
